Using Ok-Prompt

An MSX in your browser: the MSX1, MSX2 and MSX2+, with cartridges, a tape deck, a disk drive, FM sound and a modem. Everything you load stays in this browser for next time.

1Getting started

  1. Open the page and press a key or click the screen: the MSX switches on (browsers only allow sound after that). Tick Power on by itself in the Machine panel to skip this next time.
  2. The built-in machine is C-BIOS, a free MSX system ROM. It starts cartridges but has no BASIC, so with nothing in the slots it says No cartridge found.
  3. Drop a game (.rom, or a .zip with one inside) anywhere on the page. It goes in slot 1 and the MSX restarts with it.
  4. For MSX BASIC, load the ROM of a real machine (next chapter).

The panels

  • Machine (left): power, reset, save states, region, speed, the system ROM and the disk drive's ROM.
  • Centre: the monitor, two cartridge slots, the tape deck, the disk drive, the on-screen keyboard and the shelf.
  • Settings (right): display, sound and FM, keyboard and joystick port, paste, printer, modem.

Speed runs the MSX at 2x or 4x (sound is muted then). Region picks 60 Hz (NTSC, as in Japan and Brazil) or 50 Hz (PAL, Europe) on an MSX1; an MSX2 BIOS sets it itself.

2Choosing the machine

The System ROM menu decides which computer you have. The three C-BIOS machines are built in; ROMs of real machines are copyrighted, so you load dumps of your own and they stay in your browser.

Loading a BIOS

  1. Click Load BIOS… under System ROM.
  2. Pick the file, or for an MSX2 or MSX2+ both files at once (hold ⌘ or Ctrl to select two), or a zip with both.
  3. It joins the System ROM menu and the MSX restarts with it. Switch machines from the menu any time; Remove forgets the one selected.

The MSX2+ kit, step by step

  • Main ROM: main_2p_expert_com_f4.rom for an Expert, main_2p_hotbit_com_f4.rom for a Hotbit (they differ in the keyboard).
  • SUB-ROM: a com_rtc one (Ok-Prompt has the clock chip). com_fm brings MSX-MUSIC and its FM-BASIC (CALL MUSIC, PLAY #2); logo1 or logo2 is the start-up logo.
  • BASIC first comes up in SCREEN 1, because the clock's settings memory is empty. Type SCREEN 0:WIDTH 80:SET SCREEN and it remembers 80 columns from then on.

3Cartridges and the shelf

  • Click a slot or drop a file on the page to load a cartridge (.rom, .mx1, .mx2, .bin, or a .zip). The ⏏ takes it out.
  • Every cartridge, tape and disk you load goes on the shelf under the slots. Click one to put it back in slot 1, or its 2 for slot 2; × takes it off the shelf.
  • Big cartridges switch banks of memory, and makers did it differently. Ok-Prompt guesses the scheme from the code; if a game does not start, pick another in the menu under its slot: Konami, Konami SCC, ASCII 8K, ASCII 16K.
  • Games that save (role-playing games with battery-backed memory) need ASCII 8K + 8K battery RAM or ASCII 16K + 2K battery RAM in that menu. Their saves stay in the browser and come back with the cartridge.

4Keyboard, paste and joysticks

Keys type what is printed on your PC keyboard: symbols and accents go through the key table of the BIOS in use, so a Brazilian keyboard types ç, ã and " correctly on an Expert. Letters, arrows and Space keep their MSX places for games. The Keyboard setting can put every key in its MSX place instead.

Paste a program

  • Copy a BASIC listing anywhere, then click 📋 Paste text or press ⌘V (Mac) or Ctrl+Shift+V on the page.
  • Each line goes in as if typed, Enter after each, so the listing becomes a program. Then RUN.

On-screen keyboard

  • ⌨ Keyboard under the deck shows an MSX keyboard with the legends of your BIOS, for touch screens. SHIFT, CTRL, GRAPH and CODE stay down until the next key.

Joysticks and the mouse

  • Arrow keys and Space work in most games. A gamepad is joystick 1: plug it in and press a button.
  • Joystick port 1 (Settings) can hold an MSX mouse instead, for drawing programs. Click the screen to take the pointer; Esc gives it back. The left and right buttons are the mouse's.

5Tapes

  • Click the tape deck or drop a .cas or .tsx file. The deck shows the first file on the tape and its kind.
  • ▶ Load (with a BIOS that has BASIC) restarts the MSX and types the right command for you. Or type it yourself: BLOAD"CAS:",R (machine code), CLOAD then RUN (BASIC), RUN"CAS:" (BASIC as text).
  • ⏮ rewinds; the number beside the tape is how far it has played.
  • ＋ Blank inserts an empty tape: CSAVE"NAME", SAVE"CAS:NAME" or BSAVE"CAS:… record on it, and ⤓ downloads it.

Games with their own loaders

Most tapes load through the BIOS, and Ok-Prompt reads those at once. Games with their own (often faster) loaders read the sound of the tape instead: while the cassette motor runs, the deck plays the tape's signal for them, a .tsx with its own original timing. They load at the real speed, so wait as you would have in 1986.

6The disk drive

A disk drive needs the disk ROM of a real drive (it holds Disk BASIC and MSX-DOS, which are copyrighted). Load yours once and it stays in the browser. How disk drives worked, and the Brazilian models, is in the history page.

Setting it up

  1. Under Disk drive in the Machine panel, click Load disk ROM… and pick the ROM (16K, or a zip with it). The MSX restarts with a drive.
  2. A drive appears under the tape deck. Drop a .dsk on the page or click the drive; ＋ Blank inserts an empty 720K disk.
  3. An MSX1 has no clock, so the disk ROM asks for the date as it starts, as a real one did. Type it the way it asks (some want day-month-year, some month-day-year), or just press Enter.
  4. You get Disk BASIC. A disk with MSXDOS.SYS and COMMAND.COM on it boots MSX-DOS instead.

Disk BASIC in five commands

FILESLists the disk
SAVE"NAME"Saves the program (LOAD"NAME", RUN"NAME" bring it back)
BLOAD"GAME.BIN",RLoads and starts machine code
KILL"NAME.BAS"Deletes a file
CALL FORMATFormats the disk: answer A, then any key

Which disk ROMs work

  • Any plain MSX disk ROM: tested with the Philips VG-8235, Sony HB-3600, Microsol CDX-2 and FastDROM (its CDX-2 version), on the Expert 1.0 and 1.1, the Hotbit, international MSX1s and the MSX2+ kit.
  • Some EPROM dumps from Brazilian boards (DDX, TPX, DMX) are wired in a scrambled order and do not read as a disk ROM. Use the CDX-2 or FastDROM CDX-2 file for those drives: it does the same job here.
  • FastDROM leaves the most memory free.

Keeping your disks

  • Whatever the MSX writes is kept in the browser. ⤓ downloads the disk as a .dsk, to keep or open in other emulators.

7Sound and FM music

  • The MSX's own sound chip (three tones and noise) always plays; Konami SCC cartridges add their five wave channels.
  • FM sound (MSX-MUSIC), in the Sound panel, puts the Yamaha YM2413 FM chip in the machine, as in the FM-PAC cartridge and the MSX2+ machines. Games that know it play their FM music. Switching it restarts the MSX.
  • With the MSX2+ kit's com_fm SUB-ROM the chip is built in, with its FM-BASIC: CALL MUSIC, then PLAY #2,"CDEFGAB".
  • The volume slider is in the Sound panel; sound starts after the first click or key on the page.

8Save states

  • The four slots under Save states (Machine panel) each keep the whole machine: memory, chips, cartridges, the tape and the disk where they are.
  • Save stores the MSX as it is now; saving over a used slot asks first. Load brings it back, BIOS, disk ROM and cartridges included, exactly where it was.
  • A state needs its BIOS and disk ROM still loaded in the browser. States are kept in this browser only.

9The printer

LPRINT "Hello" and LLIST print on the paper in the Printer panel. ⤓ Download saves the printout as a text file; Clear starts a new page.

10The modem and the AI board

  1. Put ☎ Ok-Modem from the shelf into a slot.
  2. With MSX BASIC type CALL TERM; with C-BIOS the terminal starts by itself. On an MSX2 it shows 80 columns.
  3. ATI lists the BBSes, ATDT 1 calls the first one. You hear the dial tone and the handshake.
  4. ATDT DEEPSEEK calls the AI board: chat with the sysop or the BOFH, or order a BASIC program; it arrives on your tape for RUN"CAS:".
  5. CTRL+STOP (Ctrl+F7) goes back to BASIC.

The DeepSeek key

The AI board uses your own DeepSeek API key: paste it in the Modem panel. It stays in this browser and goes only to DeepSeek. The panel explains how to get one.

11What the browser keeps

Your BIOS and disk ROMs, the cartridges in the slots and on the shelf, the tape, the disk, battery saves, save states, settings and the MSX2 clock's memory stay in this browser, on this computer. Nothing is uploaded. Clearing the site's data (or a private window) starts afresh, so download tapes and disks you want to keep.

12When something does not work

  • A game shows a black screen or restarts: try another bank scheme in the menu under its slot.
  • No sound: click the page once; check the volume, and that Speed is Normal.
  • A tape game does not load with ▶ Load: it needs a BIOS with BASIC; C-BIOS cannot load tapes by itself.
  • The disk ROM is refused: it is probably a scrambled board dump; use a plain one (see The disk drive).
  • BASIC answers Invalid date at start-up: the disk ROM is asking for the date; type it in the order it shows, or press Enter.
  • The MSX2+ comes up in 32 columns: SCREEN 0:WIDTH 80:SET SCREEN.
  • Keys type the wrong symbols: switch the Keyboard setting between Types what your keys say and MSX key positions.
